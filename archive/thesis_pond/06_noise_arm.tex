\section{Noise arm: stochastic regeneration}
\label{sec:noise-arm}

The deterministic environment of Chapter~\ref{ch:env} has a property that
limits what any shaping experiment on it can show.
Because regeneration is deterministic, the fate of the pool is decided by
the joint action in round~1: a first-round total of at most~3 places the
stock at or above~9, from which mutual take-2 is a fixed point and every
subsequent constant action profile with total at most~4 is safe, whereas a
first-round total of~4 leaves the stock at~8, from which mutual take-2
collapses the pool in round~4 with certainty.
After a safe opening the continuation is trivial: a constant action needs no
information about the stock.
Consequently a learner has one state-dependent decision to make, a shaper
has nothing to teach beyond it, and the hawk in a hawk--dove split gains
one unit (72 against~71) from its partner's restraint.
The noise arm adds stochastic regeneration to the same integer update so
that the continuation, not only the opening, becomes a decision problem.
It is the return to the stochastic setting of the original project plan,
made after the deterministic substrate had been fixed so that every claim
could be checked against an exact solution of the game.

\subsection{Form of the noise}

Let \(R_t\) denote the post-harvest stock in round~\(t\).
The deterministic update of Chapter~\ref{ch:env} is
\(R_{t+1} = \min\bigl(K,\, R_t + g(R_t)\bigr)\) with
\(g(R) = \operatorname{round}\bigl(\rho\, R\,(K-R)/K\bigr)\), \(\rho = 0.9\)
and round-half-even rounding.
The noise arm replaces \(g\) by
\begin{equation}
  g_t(R) \;=\; \operatorname{round}\!\bigl(\xi_t\,\rho\, R\,(K-R)/K\bigr),
  \qquad
  \xi_t \in \{0.7,\;1.0,\;1.3\}\ \text{with equal probability},
  \label{eq:noise-update}
\end{equation}
where the multipliers \(\xi_t\) are independent across rounds and across
parallel games and are shared by both players within a game.
The multiplier has mean exactly one, bounded support, and is applied to the
growth increment only; the post-harvest stock itself is never perturbed.
In the implementation the multiplier is stored in tenths
(\(\xi \in \{7, 10, 13\}\)) alongside the rate in tenths, so the argument of
the rounding function is the exact rational
\(9\,\xi\,R\,(K-R)/(100K)\) and no floating-point quantity enters the update
or any prompt.

Three considerations fix this form.
First, multiplicative noise on recruitment is the standard model of growth
uncertainty in the renewable-resource literature: it is the assumption under
which Reed \cite{reed1979} derives the optimality of constant-escapement
feedback policies, and it is the ``growth uncertainty'' of the taxonomy of
Sethi et al.\ \cite{sethi2005}, as distinct from measurement and
implementation uncertainty, neither of which is studied here.
Second, multiplicative noise is proportionate at every stock level.
Additive noise of the size needed to matter at high stock (growth of~9
at \(R=20\)) would dominate the growth of one to three units at the low
stocks where the game is decided, while additive noise small enough to be
proportionate at low stock would be negligible elsewhere.
Third, because the multiplier is non-negative and multiplies only the
increment, the stock can fall only through harvest.
Collapse therefore remains attributable to the players' actions, which is
what makes survival a behavioural statistic rather than a record of
environmental luck.
Noise applied to the standing stock, as in the discarded
\texttt{stochastic\_cpr\_env.py}, and additive noise of either sign both
violate this property.
A discrete support is preferred to a lognormal multiplier because it keeps
the state space finite (41~stock levels, 36~rounds, three draws), so that
the stochastic game can be solved exactly by dynamic programming and the
properties claimed below can be verified by unit tests rather than
estimated by simulation.

\subsection{Level of the noise and the invariance argument}

The level \(\pm 30\%\) is the largest symmetric three-point multiplier at
which the constant-strategy payoff matrix of Table~\ref{tab:chicken} is
unchanged.
The two bounds are each pinned by one cell of that matrix.
Under hawk versus dove, the post-harvest stock is~5 and the deterministic
growth is \(3.94 \to 4\); with multiplier \(0.7\) the growth is
\(2.76 \to 3\), exactly the joint harvest, so the stock never falls below~8
and the pairing remains safe.
Under mutual take-2 from \(R_0 = 8\), the post-harvest stock is~4 and the
deterministic growth is \(3.24 \to 3\); with multiplier \(1.3\) the growth
is \(4.21 \to 4\), returning the stock to~8, so the profile can never
reach the fixed point at~9 and still collapses.
At \(\pm 40\%\) both bounds fail: the multiplier \(1.4\) lifts mutual take-2
to~9 with positive probability, and the multiplier \(0.6\) drives the
hawk--dove pairing below~8.
Table~\ref{tab:noise-level} shows the consequence.
At \(\pm 30\%\) all three constant-pair cells keep their deterministic
survival probabilities; at \(\pm 40\%\) and \(\pm 50\%\) mutual take-2
survives about~15\% of the time and hawk versus dove dies about~17\% of
the time, and the game is no longer the one studied in
Sections~\ref{sec:executed-fixed} and~\ref{sec:executed-two}.

Invariance of the constant-pair cells is the design property that makes
the noise arm comparable with the executed deterministic runs.
The opening decision faces the same incentives in both arms, so a change in
the opening statistic between arms is attributable to learning under
outcome variance rather than to a change in what the learner should do.
The dynamic-programming opening values against a frozen always-2 partner
confirm this: the values for openings \(0,1,2,3\) are
\(105, 104, 102, 6\) in the deterministic game and
\(102.5, 101.7, 99.1, 91.1\) under \(\pm 30\%\) noise, with the same
ordering and a gap between openings~1 and~2 of \(2.0\) and \(2.6\)
respectively.

\subsection{Structure of the stochastic game}

What the noise changes is the continuation.
Under mutual take-2 the stock evolves as a random walk on the integers with
three regions that can be read directly from the transition table:
from \(R \ge 11\) the minimum draw returns the stock to at least~11, so
mutual take-2 is safe for the rest of the horizon;
from \(R \le 7\) collapse is certain within three rounds
(\(7 \to \{5,6\}\), \(6 \to \{3,4\}\), and from~4 or below the joint
request of~4 exhausts the pool);
and \(R \in \{8, 9, 10\}\) is a gamble, since each level can move in
either direction.
The deterministic fixed point at~9 lies inside the gamble region.
A symmetric restrained opening \((1,1)\) places the stock in
\(\{9, 11, 12\}\), so ``restrain once, then mutual take-2'' survives with
probability \(0.80\); the asymmetric opening \((1,2)\) places it in
\(\{8, 9, 10\}\), so the same continuation against a hawk survives with
probability \(0.40\).
Feedback rules that condition the action on the current stock avoid the
gamble region altogether: the rule ``take~1 if \(R < 12\), else take~2''
survives with probability one against a frozen hawk, and the rule
``take~3 if \(R \ge 14\), else take~0'' survives with probability one and
achieves \(102.7\) per player when both players use it.
This is the constant-escapement structure of Reed \cite{reed1979}: under
multiplicative recruitment noise the optimal policy harvests down to a
target escapement and abstains below it.

Two rows of Table~\ref{tab:noise-dp} define the stakes for the shaping
question.
A hawk that commits to take-2 obtains~72 if its partner uses the feedback
rule and~35.7 if its partner restrains only in round~1; in the
deterministic game the corresponding figures are~72 and~72.
The noise therefore creates a 36-unit interest, on the part of a
committed hawk, in its partner's stock-conditioned restraint, where the
deterministic game offered none, and the partner's cost of providing that
restraint relative to its deterministic return is about two units
(68.8 against~71).
This is the configuration in which an opponent-shaping result of the kind
reported for the Iterated Chicken Game by Garcia Segura et al.\
\cite{garciasegura2025} is well posed: the shaper's gain from steering the
learner is large, and the learner's cost of being steered is small.

\subsection{Hypotheses and pre-registered readouts}

The arm tests three statements, each with a quantity fixed in advance by
the dynamic programme rather than by comparison with the deterministic
runs alone.

\paragraph{H1 (opening under variance).}
Because the opening incentives are invariant, a learner that responds to
them should leave opening~2 at the same rate under noise as in the
centred deterministic runs of Section~\ref{sec:executed-fixed}.
The readout is the share of episodes that do not open~2, per epoch, with
Wilson intervals.
A lower share under noise, with the deterministic value outside the
interval, measures the cost of outcome variance to credit assignment on
the opening decision.
This is the quantity that the original plan called the
signal-to-noise problem of shaping under stochastic dynamics.

\paragraph{H2 (continuation).}
Conditional on a restrained opening against a frozen hawk, survival to the
horizon is \(0.40\) if the learner continues with a constant action and
\(1.00\) if it has learned a feedback rule.
The readouts are survival conditional on a non-2 opening, and the learned
policy as a function of stock, \(\pi(a \mid R)\), estimated from the
records over the last three epochs and compared with the feedback rule in
Table~\ref{tab:noise-dp}.
An observed conditional survival near \(0.40\) with a stock-independent
\(\pi(a \mid R)\) means the learner did not condition on stock; a value
near one with \(\pi(1 \mid R < 12)\) high and \(\pi(2 \mid R \ge 12)\)
high means it did.
Intermediate values are interpreted through \(\pi(a \mid R)\), not through
survival alone.

\paragraph{H3 (shaping).}
With two learners, the quantity a committed hawk gains from its partner's
restraint is now~36 units.
The readouts are, per agent, the mean return per epoch and the
low-stock restraint probability \(\pi(1 \mid R < 12)\), compared across the
centred naive--naive and naive--shaper configurations and, when it exists,
the slow-learning-rate naive--naive control of
Section~\ref{sec:remaining-ladder}.
A shaping effect is a shaper return above the hawk-role return of the
naive--naive control and a learner restraint probability above the
naive--naive value, both across seeds; the direction predicted by the
threshold-uncertainty literature \cite{barrett2012} is that noise makes
the symmetric coordination harder, so the naive--naive control is expected
to show more collapse and more contested restraint than its deterministic
counterpart.
Absent a shaper effect, the null is reported as such.

\subsection{Protocol}

\paragraph{Runs.}
The arm re-uses the executed centred configurations with the noise update
enabled and no other change: Test~A against a frozen hawk
(three seeds), centred naive--naive (three seeds) and centred
naive--shaper (three seeds), each for 15~epochs of 15~games as in
Section~\ref{sec:executed-fixed}, or 30~epochs where compute permits,
since the return of a constant-continuation policy under noise is bimodal
(of the order of~70 if the pool lives and of~10 to~20 if it dies) and the
advantage variance is correspondingly larger.
Advantages are mean-centred, as in all executed runs; the batch-whitened
variant is not repeated on this arm because Section~\ref{sec:executed-fixed}
already shows it does not leave the opening.
Test~A is run first, since it has the sharpest theory-derived readout (H2)
and the smallest cost.

\paragraph{Common random numbers.}
The multipliers are not drawn during training.
For each seed, a table of shape (epochs, games per epoch, rounds) is drawn
once from a dedicated seed sequence, stored in the results tree, and loaded
by every configuration on the arm.
The draw used at a given (epoch, game, round) is therefore identical across
Test~A, naive--naive and naive--shaper for the same seed, and independent of
any action taken.
Between-configuration comparisons are then paired on the noise realisation,
which is the common-random-numbers design anticipated in the project plan
and used for variance reduction in Piche et al.\ \cite{piche2025}; with
three seeds it is the only design under which the H3 contrasts can be read.

\paragraph{Prompt.}
The rules text presented to the agents is unchanged if it does not state a
regeneration law; the stock is reported every round and the noise is
observable through its trajectory.
If the rules text states a deterministic law, one sentence is added
(``regrowth varies from round to round by up to 30~percent''), and the
untrained action distribution under both prompts is measured with the
pre-flight script and reported, so that a prompt-induced shift of the prior
is not mistaken for a learning effect.

\paragraph{Tests.}
Two properties are added to the test suite as exact checks against the
dynamic programme: the multiplier has mean one, and the three constant-pair
cells of Table~\ref{tab:chicken} have the survival probabilities and
expected returns of Table~\ref{tab:noise-dp} under every sequence of draws.
The regeneration function with \(\xi = 1.0\) must reproduce the
deterministic update exactly, so that the deterministic arm is the
special case of the noisy one.

\subsection{Outcome metrics}

To connect the arm to the common-pool-resource literature the project set
out from, three social-outcome metrics of the kind used by P\'erolat et
al.\ \cite{perolat2017} are reported for every configuration on both arms,
normalised against the exact optimum rather than left as raw counts:
sustainability, the survival probability to the horizon or, for collapsed
episodes, the collapse round; efficiency, the realised joint return divided
by the joint optimum (210 in the deterministic game, 206.6 under
\(\pm 30\%\) noise); and equality, \(|r_1 - r_2| / (r_1 + r_2)\).
The empirical payoff matrix of the learned policies, in the sense of
Leibo et al.\ \cite{leibo2017}, is reported alongside the constant-strategy
matrix of Table~\ref{tab:chicken} so that the two reductions can be
compared.
