\chapter{Aims}
\label{ch:aims}

This report asks whether opponent shaping, as instantiated for
language-model agents by ShapeLLM \cite{garciasegura2025}, has anything
to buy in a sequential common-pool resource, and whether it buys it.
The setting is a two-player integer logistic CPR (Chapter~\ref{ch:env})
played by Gemma-2-2b-it agents trained with PPO on rank-2 LoRA adapters
(Chapter~\ref{ch:method}), first with deterministic regeneration
(Stage~A) and then with a mean-one multiplicative shock on the growth
increment (Stage~B).
The game is small enough to be solved exactly by dynamic programming, so
every quantity a learner is compared against is a number fixed before
training, not a baseline estimated from another run.

\section{Research questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structure.}
  What does the exact solution of the game imply about what a
  prior-dominated learner can learn in it, and in particular whether a
  shaping objective has anything to buy?
  The answer bounds the effect any experiment can detect
  (Section~\ref{sec:a-to-b}) and is the reason the thesis moves from
  deterministic to stochastic regeneration.
\item \textbf{Shaping.}
  Does the trial-level opponent-shaping objective change what a naive
  learner acquires, and what the shaper earns, relative to a partner that
  is merely slow?
  This is answered by an ablation ladder that changes one ingredient of
  the partner per rung: its learning timescale, the trial-level
  objective, and the trial prompt (Section~\ref{sec:ladder}).
\item \textbf{Transfer.}
  Does a trained shaper, frozen, steer a fresh learner differently from a
  frozen hawk?
  This is ShapeLLM's claim stated for this game.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Approach}

The environment is specified as a finite Markov game whose best-response
and joint-optimum Bellman equations are solved exactly
(Chapter~\ref{ch:env}); the regeneration law is derived as the mean field
of the per-unit respawn of Commons Harvest, and the Stage~B shock as a
bounded, mean-preserving discretisation of its spread
(Appendix~\ref{app:binomial}).
The learning algorithm is written down term by term, including the
KL-to-reference penalty and the advantage operator, with every
hyperparameter and its provenance (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}).
The experiments are pre-registered: hypotheses are inequalities between
estimators and exact cells or paired controls, each with a stated null,
and the decision rules for the cases in which a check fails are fixed
before the grid runs (Chapter~\ref{ch:design}).
Replication uses independent seeds on one hardware platform and common
random numbers across arms; the fifteen-epoch runs that fixed the design
are reported as pilots, not as evidence.
Results are reported against the pre-registration
(Chapter~\ref{ch:results}), and what they do not license is stated
(Chapter~\ref{ch:lim}).

\section{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
\item An exactly solvable two-player CPR that is the mean-field
  reduction of Commons Harvest, with its Bellman equations, its
  constant-strategy reduction (chicken, not a prisoner's dilemma), and a
  derivation placing its deterministic and stochastic forms in the
  renewable-resource models of Schaefer and Reed; and the argument, from
  the exact solution, that the deterministic game bounds any shaping
  effect at one unit while the stochastic game bounds it at 36 to 70.
\item A pre-registered ablation ladder for opponent shaping between
  language-model agents, isolating the partner's stationarity, the
  trial-level objective and the trial prompt, with a transfer test and a
  one-factor sensitivity check on every hyperparameter that differs from
  ShapeLLM's release.
\item An audit of the training loop with reproducible scripts: a library
  reseed that made nominal seeds share one sampling stream, an unreported
  KL-to-prior term in the reward, and the Adam invariance that voids the
  step-size reading of advantage normalisation.
\item The results of the grid against the hypotheses, whichever way they
  fall (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope}

The commons is scalar and two-player; the spatial and exclusion
mechanisms of the gridworld commons are removed on purpose so that the
harvest decision is the only decision.
The base model is fixed; noise levels other than the executed one are
covered by the dynamic programme, not by training runs.
The training stack (Gemma-2-2b-it, rank-2 LoRA, trial PPO) is reused from
ShapeLLM with permission \cite{garciasegura2025}; the scientific object
is the environment, the design, and the contrasts.
