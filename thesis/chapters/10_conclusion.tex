\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conc}

% Written 21 September 2026 from analysis/SPINE.md sections 1, 7b and 8.

This thesis asked whether a language-model shaper, trained on the return of
a multi-episode trial while a naive co-learner adapts against it, changes
the policy that co-learner acquires --- and whether any such change is
attributable to the cross-episode objective rather than to the slower,
less frequent updates that accompany it in published work.

The answer, in a two-player stochastic commons built so that the objective
would have something to buy and verified learnable before any arm was run,
is that it does not.
On the pre-registered arms the result is a null, and it fails on the
controls: two ordinary naive learners produced an unconditional restrainer
on five of five seeds, the trial-batched control on four of five, and the
shaping arms on none.
Where the objective was estimated by chaining advantages across the trial,
it did not merely fail to teach restraint --- it extinguished the resource
on all thirteen of its seeds, leaving both agents near the value of mutual
harvest while the controls earned near the value of mutual restraint.

Three further results stand independently of that null.

\textbf{Credit assignment dominates the objective at this scale.}
Two arms identical in seat, learning rate, clip, update schedule, batch and
prompt, and differing only in how cross-episode credit is estimated, differ
in whether the resource survives at all.
An opponent-shaping objective for language-model agents is not only a choice
of objective; it is a choice of estimator, and reporting one without the
other leaves the result unidentified.

\textbf{The exploitative outcome is not diagnostic of shaping in games where
commitment pays.}
At \(m=3\) the ShapeLLM-style arm reproduced the published pattern --- agent
2 earning 1.81 units per step against 1.01, with the asymmetric profile on
0.83 of rounds --- and so did an arm whose agent 2 differs from an ordinary
learner only in learning rate and clip.
The pre-registered negative control fired, and by a rule fixed before the
data that outcome does not count as evidence for shaping.
Any claim that an objective produced such an asymmetry requires a control
holding speed and update schedule fixed.

\textbf{A gate protocol can stop an uninterpretable experiment before it is
run.}
Two of three gate attempts failed, on an unlearnable design point and on an
estimator whose advantages carried no usable signal.
Both failures are on record, and both prevented a training run whose result
could not have been interpreted either way.

\section{Broader impact}
\label{sec:conc-impact}

Opponent shaping between language-model agents is a capability to influence
how other agents learn, and its intended use and its misuse are the same
mechanism.
This experiment adds a second concern that is specific to shared resources.
The arms that optimised a shaper's own return across an interaction in which
the co-player adapts arrived at policies that destroyed the resource, and
were worse for the shaper itself than the arms that optimised only the
current episode.
Where agents trained this way are deployed on a shared and exhaustible
resource, the failure mode is not that one party loses a bargain; it is that
there is nothing left to bargain over.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

Three steps follow directly, in increasing cost.
A single diagnostic run would isolate the estimator from the co-learning
dynamics by training the chained-credit shaper against a scripted reciprocal
partner (Chapter~\ref{ch:lim}); it cannot produce a shaping result and can
only locate the failure.
A lower-variance estimator of the same trial objective, pre-registered as
the manipulated factor, would test whether the objective is reachable at all
at this scale.
And an environment, prior or budget in which the naive baseline does not
already acquire the target behaviour would restore the headroom this design
lost --- at the cost of a new pre-registration, since altering the design
point after seeing these results would convert a registered null into an
unregistered positive.
