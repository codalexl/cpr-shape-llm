\chapter{Limitations}
\label{ch:lim}

This chapter is critique.
Each section names a threat and says whether it takes down a claim
this report actually makes.

\section{Whitening as a landmine}

\textbf{Threat.}
If batch \texttt{/std} is treated as ``PPO,'' a reader will think the
death-open is a credit-assignment mystery.
It is not.
Raw opening A0 already starred 1 (\(+39.8\) vs \(+6.2\) on 2).
Whitening crushed that to \(+1.38\) vs \(+0.75\); the policy stayed on 2
(survival \(28/225\)).
Centre-only left the star (open-1 \(27\%\to 87\%\) seed~0).

\textbf{Does not take down.}
The opening claim is the \emph{centre} contrast against a frozen hawk, not
a theorem about every PPO implementation.
Whitening versus centre is machinery, not a shaping result.

\section{Frozen bot is not two learners}

\textbf{Threat.}
Centre Test~A is the strongest executed opening result and uses a partner
that does not learn.
A frozen hawk cannot be shaped.

\textbf{Does not take down.}
The report does not read Test~A as shaping.
Two-learner tables are a separate protocol.
The threat is to over-reading, which Results already forbids.

\section{Seed 2 left 2 via opening 0}

\textbf{Threat.}
Seed 2 last epoch is \(14/15\) opening 0, not 1.
If the claim were ``PPO learns take-1,'' this seed falsifies it.
Open 0 vs hawk is also a different stock path (\(8\to 11\)), not the
\(R=9\) farm.

\textbf{Does not take down.}
The licensed statistic is leave-opening-2.
Whitened Test~A never left 2 and died.
This chapter does not treat 0 and 1 as the same basin, and does not
explain why seed 2 preferred 0.

\section{What would falsify the opening claim}

A later centred hawk run, same lock, that stayed on opening 2 and died
would falsify ``centre-only advantages moved the policy off opening 2''
on this lock.
The executed contrast that already exists is the other way: whitened
stayed and died; centre left on three seeds.

\section{Two-learner contrast is not a teaching policy}

\textbf{Threat.}
Who-doves not swapping under naive--shaper looks like opponent shaping
if the reader wants it to.
The contrast is also confounded with trial update, slow LR, and cliprange.
Slow-LR naive--naive is the named isolation run and is not in this
chapter.

\textbf{Does not take down the licensed claim.}
The licensed claim is the negative: not a clear shaping success; not
two-phase (shaper leave-2 \(25/750\) on the 50-epoch seed; epoch 1 both
mostly opened 2).
Info-off raises shaper leave-2, so the extra prompt is not irrelevant
even if who-doves stays locked.
Chicken already explains a hawk--dove split.

A remaining weakness: three seeds \(\times\) 15 is small; the matched
50-epoch naive is not in this chapter unless it finishes before freeze.
That is a sample-size threat to how firmly the negative is held, not a
licence to upgrade it.

\section{Noise null (not a finding)}

The planned-arm sentence in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} is:
if stochastic regeneration leaves \(P(\mathrm{open}\ 0\mathrm{\ or}\ 1)\)
and survival indistinguishable from the deterministic centred hawk or
two-learner runs, noise is not doing the work; the basin and the update
rule are.
That sentence is a pre-registered readout, not a result.
The arm has not been run.
It is also not abandoned: it remains the stated increment until numbers
exist or Aims are re-scoped.

\section{The lock is a method fact}

\((R_0,K,T,\texttt{rate\_tenths})=(8,40,36,9)\) is a method lock from the
integer logistic scout, not a literature calibration.
Reed (1979) is an aim-column citation for planned noise on this same
update (publisher abstract; PDF not obtained).
It does not set those four integers (Chapter~\ref{ch:env}).
