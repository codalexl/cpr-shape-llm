\chapter{Limitations}
\label{ch:lim}

These are the constraints that bound what the design of
Chapter~\ref{ch:design} can show, stated before the grid's results; the
limitations that the results themselves impose are added in
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} against each hypothesis.

\section{What the exact solution bounds}

A shaping effect cannot exceed \eqref{eq:effect-bound}.
In Stage~A the bound is one unit, so a null there is by design and a
positive result would be an artefact; the deterministic arms exist to
show the predicted null, not to detect shaping.
In Stage~B the bound is conditional on C2: if a naive learner acquires
the feedback rule alone, the control partner already collects most of
what the shaper could, and the detectable effect shrinks to a difference
in speed and reliability.
The design measures speed (first majority) for that reason, but three to
five seeds resolve a speed difference only if it is large.

\section{Replication}

The pilots of Section~\ref{sec:pilot-results} were run before the trainer
reseed was found (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}); their nominal seeds shared
one action-sampling stream, so every ``on all three seeds'' statement
about them is closer to one replicate plus hardware noise than to three
draws, and the seed-fixed pilots showed the fifteen-epoch shaper
contrast to be mixed in sign once the seeds were independent.
The grid runs three seeds per arm and five on the Stage~B ladder.
With so few seeds the evidence is sign agreement and effect size against
exact cells; no test statistic is reported, and a positive result on
five seeds is a direction with a bound, not a law.
The early pilots mixed Apple-silicon and L40S provenance; the grid runs
on one platform, and the whitened seed-0 pilot that stayed on opening 2
confounds seed with platform and is not a result.

\section{The learning loop}

The reward carries an adaptive KL penalty to the reference model
\eqref{eq:klreward} with coefficient about \(0.2\); it is a restoring
force towards the digit prior that every result is obtained under, and
its coefficient is one of the parameters whose sensitivity is checked
one factor at a time, not swept.
Adam makes the advantage operator a reweighting of the policy gradient
against the critic, the entropy bonus and the other batches rather than a
step size (Section~\ref{sec:operator}); the executed runs do not identify
which of those weights matters, and the design does not try to.
Several hyperparameters are this project's own choices rather than
ShapeLLM's (Table~\ref{tab:provenance}); the sensitivity block checks
each on the cheapest arm only, so an interaction between them on the
two-learner arms is not excluded.
The shaper receives one update per trial, 100 in the grid; the transfer
test trains the fresh learner for 30 epochs.
Both are budgets, and a shaper that needs more updates than that to
express its objective would be missed.

\section{Statistics}

Leave-2 is a statistic of a near-constant-policy class: after a
restrained opening against a hawk the tape is mostly take-2 whether or
not the learner has acquired a stock-conditioned rule, which is why the
low-stock restraint share and \(\hat{\pi}(a\mid R)\) are the primary
continuation readouts and the opening share is not.
Windows of the last twenty epochs average over whatever drift remains;
the stationarity criterion is a one-standard-error rule, not a
convergence proof.

\section{The environment}

The commons is scalar and two-player, with the exclusion mechanism of
the gridworld commons removed; a mechanism that produced sustainability
there cannot appear here.
Stage~B's multiplier is a bounded discretisation of the per-unit process,
chosen for invariance of the constant-pair cells; under the exact
binomial kernel those cells move (Appendix~\ref{app:binomial}), so the
results are about growth uncertainty of a specific, mild form.
The prompt does not state the regeneration law; the learner sees the
stock every round and must infer the law from its trajectory.
Both players observe the stock exactly and requests are filled exactly,
so measurement and implementation uncertainty in the sense of Sethi et
al.\ are absent by construction.
The joint optimum harvests six units per round below the maximum
sustainable yield because the per-player cap is three; a larger action
set would change the game's optimum and possibly its class.
