\chapter{Environment}
\label{ch:env}

% Written 21 September 2026 from analysis/SPINE.md section 3 and docs/DIAL_ARMS.md.

The experiment needs a game in which a co-player's restraint is worth
something to a shaper whatever the shaper's own competence, in which no
player can secure the resource alone, and which is small enough to solve
exactly so that every quantity an agent is compared against is fixed before
training.
This chapter defines that game, solves it, and states the two regimes that
make one of them a negative control.
Nothing here is a training result.

\section{Definition}
\label{sec:env-def}

Two players share an integer stock and act simultaneously for a fixed
horizon of \(T=50\) rounds.
The game is the finite Markov game
\(\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,c,T,R_1)\) with
\(\mathcal{S}=\{0,\dots,K\}\), \(K=20\), \(\mathcal{A}=\{1,2\}\) and
\(R_1=K\): every episode starts with a full pool.
Writing \(A_t=a_{1,t}+a_{2,t}\) for the joint request, receipts and the
post-harvest stock are
\begin{equation}
  (c_{1,t},c_{2,t},\tilde{R}_t)=
  \begin{cases}
    (a_{1,t},\,a_{2,t},\,R_t-A_t) & A_t\le R_t,\\[2pt]
    \bigl(\min(a_{1,t},\lfloor R_t/2\rfloor),\,
          \min(a_{2,t},\lfloor R_t/2\rfloor),\,0\bigr) & A_t>R_t,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:dial-harvest}
\end{equation}
so a joint request the stock cannot meet empties it and wastes the
remainder.
A strictly positive post-harvest stock regrows by the rounded logistic
increment, scaled by a multiplicative shock, and an empty pool is absorbing:
\begin{equation}
  R_{t+1}=
  \begin{cases}
    \min\bigl(K,\ \tilde{R}_t+g(\tilde{R}_t;\xi_t)\bigr) & \tilde{R}_t>0,\\
    0 & \tilde{R}_t=0,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  g(R;\xi)=\operatorname{round}\Bigl(\xi\,\rho\,\frac{R(K-R)}{K}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:dial-growth}
\end{equation}
with half-even rounding and \(\xi_t\) drawn independently each round from
\(\{0.7,1.0,1.3\}\), equally likely, shared by both players and taken from a
per-seed table that every arm reuses.
In code the argument is an exact rational, so no floating-point value
reaches a prompt or a record.
Player \(i\)'s return is \(G_i=\sum_{t=1}^{T}c_{i,t}\), and an episode
\emph{survives} if the pool is non-empty when the horizon is reached.
A round is masked only if the pool was already empty at its start; the
collapse round itself is a real decision and is scored.

\paragraph{The shock.}
The multiplier is a bounded, mean-one, three-point discretisation of
per-unit stochastic regrowth: if each unit of surviving stock recruits
independently, the increment is binomial with the logistic term of
\eqref{eq:dial-growth} as its mean, and the multiplier approximates its
spread while keeping the state space finite and the dynamic programme exact.
The level of \(\pm 30\) per cent is inherited from the predecessor design
(Section~\ref{sec:env-pond}), where it was the largest symmetric
three-point spread that left the constant-play cells unchanged.
In the terms of Reed \cite{reed1979} and Sethi et al.\ \cite{sethi2005}
this is growth uncertainty alone: both players observe the stock and both
requests exactly, and the shock is never applied to an empty pool.

\paragraph{The dial.}
One parameter separates two regimes: \(\rho\), stored as
\texttt{rate\_tenths} 5 or 6.
Write \(m\) for the peak integer growth at \(\xi=1\), which is 2 at
\(\rho=0.5\) and 3 at \(\rho=0.6\).
Nothing else differs between them --- prompts, tokens, horizon, stock,
shock table and every hyperparameter are identical --- so a contrast between
regimes isolates the growth rate.

\paragraph{No abstain action.}
The action set is \(\{1,2\}\) and there is no take-0.
With an abstain action available, committed harvest at \(m=2\) earns 100.0
with survival 1.00, because one player can keep the pool alive by taking
nothing; that restores exactly the escape this design exists to remove.

\section{Exact solution}
\label{sec:env-solution}

The state space is 21 stocks by 50 rounds with three shocks per transition,
so values are computed exactly by backward induction rather than estimated
by simulation (\texttt{cpr\_dial.py}).
Against a fixed partner policy the value of the optimal reply satisfies
\begin{equation}
  V^{\sigma}(R,t)=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
  \Bigl\{c_1\bigl(R,a,\sigma(R,t)\bigr)
    +\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\bigl[V^{\sigma}(R',t+1)\bigr]\Bigr\},
  \qquad V^{\sigma}(\cdot,T+1)=0,
  \label{eq:dial-bellman}
\end{equation}
with \(R'\) given by \eqref{eq:dial-harvest}--\eqref{eq:dial-growth}.
Best responses within the stationary class are found by coordinate ascent
from five starts, so the values reported for them are lower bounds on what a
stationary policy can achieve, which is the direction that matters: they
understate rather than overstate what an agent could have done.
\texttt{tests/test\_cpr\_dial.py} pins every number below.

\section{Why this lock can host the question}
\label{sec:env-invariants}

\paragraph{I2, no unilateral salvation \((m=2)\).}
The best expected drift of restraint against a harvesting partner is
\(-0.67\) units a round, and a lone restrainer never reaches the horizon
with a live pool (survival 0.00).
Restraint pays only if it is sustained by both players.

\paragraph{I3, commitment fails \((m=2)\).}
Against a best-responding partner, committed harvest earns 31.5 while
tit-for-tat earns 62.5.
A restrained partner is worth 55.1 units to a best responder, and that
figure does not depend on the shaper first learning to manage the stock,
because no policy can manage it alone.

\paragraph{Payoff degeneracy, stated as a property.}
Exploiting an always-restrainer earns 75.2 while the restrainer still earns
50.0, the value of mutual restraint; mutual harvest earns 17.8.
In one-shot terms \(T=75.2>R=S=50.0>P=17.8\), so a restrainer pays nothing
for being exploited.
Restraint is therefore cheap for the co-learner and does not by itself
discriminate shaping, which is why the readout of Chapter~\ref{ch:method} is
the shape of the acquired policy and not the amount of restraint.

\paragraph{\(m=3\), the negative control.}
At the higher growth rate both invariants fail: committed harvest earns 90.7
against tit-for-tat's 78.3, so commitment wins, and a lone restrainer keeps
the pool 0.80 of the time.
An exploitative asymmetry there is what commitment predicts, which is what
makes the regime a control rather than a second experiment
(Chapter~\ref{ch:design}, decision C).

\paragraph{What the toy gate adds.}
A two-parameter learner played against three scripted partners --- committed
harvest, the best fixed rule, and a learning-aware schedule --- ends in three
different policy classes at \(m=2\).
That licenses reading policy identity as the primary outcome.
It does not predict that PPO on LoRA adapters will find the learning-aware
schedule, which is the experiment.

\section{What the agents read}
\label{sec:env-prompt}

Both seats receive the same rules string, rendered from structured state;
no prompt is ever parsed from a previous prompt.
The horizon is never stated and there is no round counter, so a stationary
policy conditioned on the stock and the previous round is expressible.
At the start of an episode:

\begin{quote}\small
\begin{verbatim}
You are interacting with another agent. Each round you choose how many
units to take from a shared resource (1 or 2). You receive exactly the
number of units you successfully take. If the two requests together
exceed the resource, both of you are cut back and the remainder is lost.
After each round the resource regrows by an amount that varies by up to
30 percent from round to round, but once it reaches zero it never
recovers. Your goal is to maximise the total units you collect.
Current resource level: 20.
Reply with only one number: 1 or 2.
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}

From the second round a previous-round line reports requests and receipts
separately, so scarcity is visible:

\begin{quote}\small
\begin{verbatim}
Current resource level: 14.
In the previous round you requested 1 and received 1; the other agent
requested 2 and received 2.
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}

The rules state that regrowth varies but not the law that governs it, so an
agent must infer the dynamics from its trajectory.
One arm removes the previous-round line from both seats, and one gives
agent~2 two further lines that persist across episodes within a trial: the
joint-request counts so far, and a one-line summary of each finished
episode.
Those are the only prompt differences in the experiment
(Chapter~\ref{ch:design}).

\section{The predecessor design, and why it was replaced}
\label{sec:env-pond}

The project began on a larger stock of the same integer family
(\(R_0=8\), \(K=40\), \(T=36\), \(\rho=0.9\), takes 0--3).
That lock cannot host the question.
Regrowth there peaks at nine units a round against a joint harvest of at
most six, so an agent that manages the stock keeps it alive against almost
any partner: a restrained partner is worth 2.0 units to a best responder
from the starting stock and 0.0 from a full one.
One restraint rescues a falling pool, so a slow partner and a shaper that
never restrains produce the same outcome, and any measured stake is a
measure of the shaper's own incompetence.
The design above removes each of those escapes by construction.
