\begin{abstract}
Opponent shaping trains an agent to influence how a co-player learns.
ShapeLLM showed that a language-model shaper, trained on the return of a
multi-episode trial, reaches the exploitative outcome of iterated Chicken
against a naive language-model learner.
That shaper also learns an order of magnitude more slowly and updates five
times less often than its co-player, and in Chicken a slower agent behaves
more like a committed one, so the outcome has two candidate causes that the
published design does not separate.
This thesis asks the same question in a two-player stochastic common-pool
resource whose exact solution fixes what a co-player's restraint is worth,
and separates the causes with a control that shares the shaper's update
schedule, learning rate, clip, batch and prompt but not its cross-episode
objective.
Agents are Gemma-2-2b-it with rank-2 LoRA adapters trained by PPO.
Across 43 pre-registered training runs in two growth regimes, the
trial-level objective did not change the policy the co-learner acquired: two
naive learners produced an unconditional restrainer on every seed, and the
shaping arms did not.
Where the objective was estimated by chaining advantages across the trial,
it extinguished the resource on every seed, both agents earning near the
mutual-harvest value, while the controls sustained it.
An arm differing only in how that credit is estimated sustained the resource
and matched the controls.
The exploitative outcome the literature reports appeared only in the regime
where a partner with no shaping objective produced it too.
\end{abstract}
