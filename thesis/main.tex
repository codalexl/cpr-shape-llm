\begin{abstract}
This report asks whether opponent shaping, as instantiated for
language-model agents by ShapeLLM, has anything to buy in a sequential
common-pool resource, and whether it buys it.
The environment is a two-player integer logistic stock
(\(R_0=8\), \(K=40\), \(T=36\), \(\rho=0.9\)), the mean-field reduction
of the Commons Harvest gridworld, small enough to be solved exactly:
under constant strategies it is a chicken game, and its best-response
and joint-optimum Bellman equations fix every value a learner is compared
against.
Agents are Gemma-2-2b-it with rank-2 LoRA adapters trained by PPO, with
the naive and trial-level (shaping) schedules of ShapeLLM, reused with
permission.

The exact solution shows that in the deterministic game a committed hawk
gains at most one unit from anything a shaper could induce, so a shaping
experiment there is unpowered by construction; under a mean-one
multiplicative shock on the growth increment, chosen so that the
constant-strategy cells are invariant, the hawk's stake in its partner's
stock-conditioned restraint is 36 units and its ceiling 105.5.
The experiments are pre-registered as an ablation ladder that changes one
ingredient of the partner per rung (learning timescale, trial-level
objective, trial prompt), with a transfer test of the frozen shaper
against a fresh learner, hypotheses stated as inequalities against exact
cells with paired controls and common random numbers, and a one-factor
sensitivity check on every hyperparameter that differs from ShapeLLM's
release.
Pilot runs fixed the design and exposed three properties of the training
loop: a library reseed that made nominal seeds share one sampling
stream, an unreported KL-to-prior term in the reward, and the Adam
invariance that voids the step-size reading of advantage normalisation.
