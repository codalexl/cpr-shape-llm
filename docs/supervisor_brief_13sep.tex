\section*{Question}
Does opponent shaping, as instantiated for language-model agents by ShapeLLM, have anything to buy in a sequential common-pool resource, and does it buy it?
The game is solved exactly, so every comparison value is fixed before training.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structure.} What can a prior-dominated learner learn, and does a shaping objective have anything to buy?
\item \textbf{Shaping.} Does the trial-level objective change what a naive learner acquires, and what the shaper earns, relative to a partner that is merely slow?
\item \textbf{Transfer.} Does a trained shaper, frozen, steer a fresh learner differently from a frozen hawk?
\end{enumerate}

\section*{The game}
Two players, simultaneous requests \(a_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), horizon \(T=36\).
Reward is units received that step (undiscounted sum).
All arithmetic is integer; no float reaches a prompt.
Lock: \(R_0=8\), \(K=40\), \(\rho=0.9\) (\texttt{rate\_tenths}=9).

A step:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Both request.
\item If \(a_1+a_2\le R\): each receives its request and \(R\leftarrow R-(a_1+a_2)\).
  Else (scarcity): each receives \(\min(a_i,\lfloor R/2\rfloor)\); remainder wasted; \(R\leftarrow 0\).
\item If \(R>0\): regenerate, capped at \(K\).
  Else: \(R\) stays 0 (absorbing zero). A dead pool pays 0 for the rest of the horizon.
\end{enumerate}
Exact depletion (\(a_1+a_2=R\)) is the normal branch: it drives \(R\) to 0, then absorbing zero kills regeneration.
The stock falls only through harvest.

Growth after a strictly positive post-harvest stock \(S\) is rounded logistic surplus,
\[
  R'=\min\bigl(K,\,S+\mathrm{round\_half\_even}(\xi\,\rho\,S(K-S)/K)\bigr),
\]
with the argument the exact rational in tenths (not binary float).
Stage~A is \(\xi\equiv 1\).
Stage~B draws \(\xi\) i.i.d.\ on \(\{0.7,1.0,1.3\}\), common to both players, never applied at \(S=0\).
At this lock there is no interior freeze: \(R=1\) still grows by 1.

From \(R=8\) against a take-2 partner, the first action is the fork:
open~1 moves \(8\to 9\), after which mutual take-2 is a live farm;
open~2 stays at 8 and dies around round~4;
open~0 moves \(8\to 11\), not onto that farm.
Claim statistics are openings (share that are not 2) and survival, not the live-step mix after a save.

Linear ``\(+2\) per round'' is retired: against a grabber, take-1 and take-2 paid the same.
Agents: Gemma-2-2b-it, rank-2 LoRA, PPO, one-token actions; untrained prior opens 2 about 85\% of the time.

\section*{Exact solution (not a training result)}
The state is 41 stocks \(\times\) 36 rounds, so the Bellman equations for best response and joint optimum are solved exactly by backward induction (\texttt{cpr\_xi.py}; tests pin every cell).
A GPU run is compared with these cells, not the other way around.
Every specific decimal below that is not labelled as a training return is from this table.

``Hawk'' is always-2.
``Restrain-once'' is open 1 then 2 forever.
``Feedback'' is take 1 if \(R<12\), else 2.
Returns are player~1 / player 2.

\vspace{-0.3em}
The first three rows are \textbf{chicken, not a prisoner's dilemma}.
Constant pairs are invariant under \(\xi\).
Open-then-constant is not: hawk vs restrain-once is safe in Stage~A and a gamble under \(\xi\).
Feedback stays safe.

\paragraph{Why two stages.}
A shaping effect cannot exceed what the shaper earns against the partner it induces, minus what it earns against the partner a control induces.
In Stage~A that difference is at most one unit (hawk gets 72 against both restrain-once and feedback).
Under \(\xi\) the continuation is a decision: 72 against feedback, 35.7 against restrain-once, 7.44 against mutual take-2.
If the naive learner cannot acquire feedback alone, the hawk-side bound is \(72-35.7=36\); if the shaper exploits a feedback dove rather than staying on 2, the ceiling is 105.5.
C2 decides which bound applies to H-B.

\section*{Design}
Agent~1 is always naive.
The ladder changes one ingredient of agent~2 per rung.
100~epochs, seeds 0/1/2, L40S, TRL default (whitened) advantages, common random numbers across Stage~B arms.

\textbf{C1}: Stage~B Test~A leave-2 (last 20 epochs) lies in Stage~A's Wilson interval, seed by seed.
\textbf{C2}: vs a frozen hawk under \(\xi\), leave-2 at \(R<12\ge 0.5\) and survival given a leave-2 opening \(>0.40\) within 100 epochs.
\textbf{H-A}: in Stage~A, naive--shaper vs slow-LR moves the shaper's return by at most one unit.
\textbf{H-B1--3}: each rung raises shaper return and learner feedback share (sign agreement across seeds).
\textbf{H-B4}: shaper return exceeds the plain-hawk cell 72, toward the DP ceiling 105.5.
\textbf{H-T}: a fresh learner acquires feedback faster or more often against the frozen shaper than against the frozen hawk.
A null is reported as a null.
Fifteen-epoch runs that fixed the design (including a library reseed that had made nominal seeds share one sampling stream) are pilots, not evidence.
