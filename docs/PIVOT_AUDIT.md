# Audit of the stochastic-CPR pivot (14 September 2026, before any environment code)

Scope: `docs/PREREGISTRATION_STOCHASTIC_CPR.md`, `cpr_dial.py`, `tests/test_cpr_dial.py`, `results/dial/design_report.txt`, read against `review_round4.md`, the pond grid, and the timeline to 30 September. Every number below is from `cpr_dial.py` run today.

## 1. Verdict

The pivot is scientifically right and the design is the best-specified thing in the repository. Round 4's central point stands: in the pond a competent agent keeps the pool alive against almost any partner, so the partner's policy is worth 1 to 2 units to it, and the 36-unit stake exists only for an agent that stays on take-2. The pond therefore tests whether a shaper stays near its prior and benefits from a restrained partner, not whether shaping produces anything a competent fixed strategy cannot. The dial fixes exactly that: at m = 2 one player cannot carry the pool (I2), commitment loses to reciprocity against a best responder (I3), and the partner's policy is worth 40 units to *any* shaper. The m = 3 negative control, in which the same family becomes the pond's regime, is the cleanest part of the design. Go, subject to the changes in Section 3, all of which are cheap, and the risk in Section 4, which is not.

## 2. What holds

- **The invariants are exact and reproduce.** I2, I3 and the restraint-worth at both design points and both K = 30 replications match the pre-registration; the nine tests pass. The dial is a single parameter, and nothing else differs between regimes.
- **The claim sentence is falsifiable and each clause has an arm.** "Could not have produced by a good fixed rule" → E2 replay and the gate's best fixed rule; "by learning more slowly" → the slow arm; "by reacting only to the current stock" → the stock-band learner and the probe's r_low / r_high; "a frozen copy does the same" → E1.
- **Policy identity as the primary readout is the right call.** The gate's learning-aware gain (6 to 13 per episode) is below the pond's between-seed SD of returns (≈16.5); the restraint-probability gaps (≈0.9) are eight times the between-seed SD of a restraint share. The probe evaluation (a scripted partner, no updates) measures the partner's policy independently of who trained it, which the pond never had.
- **E2 (replay) and E4 (partner cannot learn)** are the two controls that separate shaping from a good open-loop schedule and from competence; they are what M-FOS and Shaper use, and the pond grid lacks them.
- **The random end** removes the end-game grab and makes the agents' information set the solver's. Mean 34.3 rounds keeps the budget close to the pond's.
- **The go/no-go gate on 17 September** with "no training on this design if G1 or G2 fails" is the right risk control: the thesis stays submittable on the pond.

## 3. What to change before writing environment code

1. **The trial-batched naive is not optional.** The 2×2 as written (naive, slow, shaper-matched, shaper-slow) compares a shaper that updates once per trial with a naive learner that updates five times per trial, so "objective without timescale" (shaper-matched minus naive) still conflates cross-episode credit with the update schedule and with five times fewer Adam steps. This is the confound the pond ladder was amended to remove on 13 September; the pivot reintroduces it. Fix: the control for each shaper arm is a trial-batched naive at the same learning rate and clip (`trial_batched=true`, already implemented and smoke-tested on the pond). Arms at m = 2: naive, slow, tbn-matched, shaper-matched, tbn-slow, shaper-slow, ShapeLLM. Objective effect = shaper-matched − tbn-matched and shaper-slow − tbn-slow; timescale effect = slow − naive and tbn-slow − tbn-matched. Cost: two more arms × 3 seeds × ≈1.4 h ≈ 8 GPU-h. Drop the two m = 3 arms that add nothing to the negative control (m = 3 needs naive, slow and ShapeLLM only) to pay for it.
2. **G1 at m = 2 is satisfied by the untrained prior.** The criterion "restraint rate below 0.2 against always-harvest" is met at epoch 0 (prior restraint ≈ 0.10), so it gates nothing. The learning that G1 should demonstrate at m = 2 is the best response to a doomed pool, which is grab: replace with "grab share at stock ≥ 12 at least 0.5 in most seeds" (prior ≈ 0.04), or drop the m = 2 half and keep the m = 3 half (yielding ≥ 0.8), which does require learning.
3. **Add one full-length shaper pilot to the gate.** G2 shows a learner can learn reciprocity when handed a tit-for-tat partner. Nothing in the gate shows a *shaper* can discover a teaching rule from a 0.85-on-harvest prior while its partner also harvests: under the untrained pair the pool is dead by round 10 with probability 0.92, so almost no experience of a live pool at mutual restraint is ever generated. Run shaper-matched (or ShapeLLM) versus naive at m = 2, one seed, 100 epochs, on the 17th in parallel with G1/G2 (≈1.4 h). If the shaper's restraint rate never leaves the prior, the design needs a lever (longer training, or a warmer prompt measured by G0) before 30 runs are spent. This is the largest risk in the pivot (Section 4).
4. **State the payoff degeneracy.** At m = 2 the best response to an always-restrainer earns 57.1 with survival 1.00 while the restrainer still earns 36.0, the mutual-restraint value. A restrainer therefore pays nothing for being exploited (T = 57 > R = 36 = S = 36 > P = 16). This is the pond's "the dove earns 36 either way" in a milder form. It makes the *yielding* half of the shaping signature cheap for the learner and should be written into the pre-registration as a property, with the consequence that the discriminating readout is r_after_take (restraint even when not reciprocated), not restraint as such. The gate already relies on this; say so.
5. **Make the class rule robust to sparse cells.** Under the probe (grab with probability 1/2) the pool at m = 2 dies within a few rounds, so r_high and r_after_restrain may rest on few rounds per seed. State a minimum count per rate (for example 30 rounds) below which the rate is "undetermined" rather than a class, and report the counts.
6. **Seeds.** Three seeds with "most = 2 of 3" means one seed decides a class flip. Run five at m = 2 on the arms that carry decision S (shaper-matched, ShapeLLM, tbn-matched, naive); three elsewhere. The budget allows it once the m = 3 arms are trimmed.
7. **Episode-length variance.** Returns now vary with the drawn length (mean 34, up to 108). Report per-round rates as well as per-episode returns, and note that with γ = 1 the value head must learn the expected remaining length; the geometric end makes that a constant, which is the point of the design, but say it.

## 4. Implementation pitfalls (the code does not exist yet; these are the places it will break)

- **Actions 1–3.** `CPRDynamics` asserts requests in 0..n_actions−1 and `TokenToActionMapper` maps token index to take; the dial needs an offset (index i → take i+1) applied in exactly one place, with a test that the three tokens map to takes 1, 2, 3 and that no take-0 can be emitted. `cpr_xi`, `cpr_eval.pi_given_R` (four action columns) and every "leave-2" readout assume the pond's action set; the dial needs its own readouts (r_after_restrain, r_after_take, r_low, r_high) in the evaluator, with tests on simulated policies as before.
- **The launcher lock.** `run_cpr.sh` refuses any config that is not (8, 40, 36, 9). It needs a second accepted point per regime, asserted as strictly as the first.
- **Random end.** A per-seed closing-round table shared across arms, like the noise table; rounds after the close masked with the same NaN mechanism as post-collapse rounds; a record flag distinguishing "closed with the pool alive" from "collapsed", because survival is now the first, not the absence of the second. Memory: a trial can reach 5 × 108 × 3 = 1620 positions, three times the pond's; smoke one run with the close forced to 108 on every episode before launching (the pond OOM was at 24 GiB on a 44 GiB card).
- **Bots need state.** Tit-for-tat, the probe and the replay bot need the partner's last request and the stock; `ConstantActionAgent` sees nothing. Give bots a structured observation from `CPRGame` (not the prompt string), and pin "round 0 counts as mutual restraint" in a test against `cpr_dial.tit_for_tat`.
- **Replay bot.** The learner in E2 changes when its pool dies, so the recorded tape and the live episode differ in length; fix a rule (replay by round index, hold the last take past the tape's end) and record it.
- **Evaluation-only mode.** E3, E4 and the probes need both agents frozen (learning rate 0 is not enough if the trainer still steps: use the `FrozenAdapterAgent` path for both).
- **Prompt.** The text is new and must be preflighted (G0) in both regimes; the prior is measured, and the same three tokens are used in both regimes.
- **Seeds and CRN.** The seed fix, the epoch-1 gate and the per-seed tables carry over unchanged; the closing-round table joins them.

## 5. Timeline and what the pond ladder means for it

The pond ladder (naive–shaper, info-off, trial-batched, slow-LR; five seeds × 200 epochs) finished today; naive–naive was not run. Its evaluation is the pond's result and is written up regardless of the pivot, as the pre-registration says. The pivot's timeline (code 15–16, gate 17, training 18–20, evaluation 21, analysis 22, writing 23–28) has no slack: any day lost to a bug in the new paths is a day taken from writing. Two rules follow. The pond chapters must be submission-ready by 22 September on their own, with the dial as an added chapter. And nothing launches on the 18th unless the 17th's gate, including the shaper pilot of Section 3.3, has passed and been logged in the amendment section.

## 6. What the pivot does not fix

The learner is still Gemma-2-2b-it with an adaptive KL penalty to a prior that puts 0.85 on the harvest token, in both regimes; the cross-episode credit is still λ-attenuated (0.97^36 ≈ 0.33 one episode later); three seeds remain the unit of evidence; and the toy gate says what the environment can separate, not what PPO on LoRA will find. All four are stated in the pre-registration and belong in the thesis's limitations unchanged.
