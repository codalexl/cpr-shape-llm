## 4. Results the draft refuses to state

These are the substantive findings in the new data. Each is checkable against the DP, and each is currently either missing or explicitly declined.

**The noise arm answered H1 and H2, and the answer is in your own table.** Under noise, Test A seeds 0 and 2 opened 0 on 15/15 games in the last epoch, where Stage A had opened 1. Chapter 7 says "This chapter does not write a why-sentence on 15×0." The why is one line: against a hawk, opening 0 places the stock in {9, 11, 12} and opening 1 in {8, 9, 10}; a constant take-2 continuation then survives with probability 0.80 from the first set and 0.40 from the second, with expected returns 58.3 and 34.7. The learner moved to the opening that doubles its survival, which is the DP-optimal direction (Q(0) > Q(1) under noise), and the observed last-epoch returns, 65.5 and 58.3 on the open-0 seeds against 26.9 on the mixed seed, sit where the DP puts them. That is H1 confirmed in a stronger form than pre-registered (the learner not only kept leaving 2, it chose the better exit) and H2 answered in the negative (leave-2 at R<12 of 28–41% and returns far below the feedback rule's 69 mean it did not learn to condition on stock). This is the arm's main result and it is currently one refused sentence.

**The shaper versus slow-control contrast under noise is not "same qualitative".** Paired on the same noise tables, whole-run survival was 90, 71, 75 under naive–shaper and 53, 44, 39 under slow-LR naive–naive; the naive's leave-2 in the last three epochs was 96, 93, 89% against the shaper and 69, 67, 40% against the slow control. Agent 1 is identical in the two arms. The difference is that the shaper is a more consistent hawk (0–11% leave-2 versus 18–20%), and a more consistent hawk trains a more reliable dove. That is the ShapeLLM chicken mechanism in miniature: a committed Go produces a Swerve. It is confounded with the tighter clip range, which also makes a policy more stationary, so the defensible sentence is "a more stationary hawk produced a more reliable dove on all three paired seeds; whether the trial update or the clip range supplies the stationarity is not separated." That is a finding with a stated confound, not a null. It also cannot be evaluated against your pre-registered H3 because returns for the naive–naive and slow-control arms are not reported anywhere; report them.

**The matched long naive found the higher-yield action and out-earned the shaper.** At epoch 50, the naive pair opened (1,1) and agent 2 earned 98.1 by taking 3, which is the (1,1)-then-(2,3) fixed point at R=11 with DP value 71/106. The pair earned about 167; the shaper pair at epoch 50 earned about 143 with the shaper on 72. Under the H3 criterion you pre-registered (shaper return above the hawk-role return of the control), the shaper is 26 units below. One seed each, so it is a direction not a result, but it is the only run in the thesis where a learner found a policy above the constant chicken cells, and it is currently reported in one line followed by "Not a 36-step dove policy."

**RQ2 has changed under your own data and the conclusion has not caught up.** Whitened seeds 1–2 left opening 2 (returns 69.5, 69.7). So the tally is whitened 1/3 stays, centred 0/3 stays, Fisher exact p = 1.0. Chapter 9 still says "centre-only advantages left opening 2; batch whitening did not", which is now false by Chapter 7. The honest answer to RQ2 is that the two operators are not distinguishable by outcome at n=3, and the interesting question becomes mechanistic. Whitening and centring differ by a factor 1/σ_B, the batch standard deviation of the raw advantages, and your own numbers imply σ_B ≈ 53 in the whitened snapshot. That standard deviation is inflated by the large raw GAE on later steps of surviving games, which is what a near-untrained critic (vf_coef = 0.01, γ = 1) produces. So at a fixed learning rate of 1.41 × 10⁻⁶, whitening makes every opening-step update roughly fifty times smaller than centring does, and whether a whitened run escapes the prior within 15 epochs becomes a slow random walk that some seeds complete and some do not. In other words, "centring works" may be the same statement as "a fifty-fold larger learning rate works", and the returns in this game are an order of magnitude larger than in ShapeLLM's matrix games, which is why ShapeLLM's learning rate was tuned for the whitened scale. An examiner who knows PPO will think of this within a minute of reading Chapter 4. The one-run ablation that settles it is whitened Test A with the learning rate multiplied by about 50 (three seeds if you can); if it escapes like the centred runs, RQ2's answer is "a step-size effect, not a sign effect", which is a cleaner and more general finding than the current one. Either way, plot the per-epoch A0 for all six existing runs and use the logit-update equation in the framework file to say what each seed's batch did.

**Noise broke symmetric coordination on one seed.** Naive–naive under noise, seed 1: both agents still mostly opened 2 at epoch 15, survival 43/225, where all three Stage A naive–naive seeds had left 2. That is the direction the threshold-uncertainty literature predicts and it belongs in the results with that framing.
