# Review of "Opponent shaping for language-model agents in a sequential common-pool resource" (draft of 5 Sept 2026)

Reviewer stance: principal research scientist reading as an internal examiner would. I read all nine chapters, `main.tex`, and the public repo at HEAD, and I re-implemented the environment from Chapter 3 to check the game-theoretic claims. My dynamic-programming values reproduce your Table 3 payoffs and your DP opening values Q = (105, 104, 102, 6) exactly, so the analysis below is about the game you actually built.

## Overall verdict

There is a defensible MSc thesis inside this draft, but it is not the one the draft says it is, and the draft is not yet written for a reader. The strongest material is the advantage-normalisation finding (default TRL whitening keeps a peaked-prior LLM policy on a catastrophic opening; mean-centring lets it leave), backed by a clean environment with a fully solvable structure. The weakest material is the opponent-shaping story, which by the report's own admission is a null with a named, unrun control, and which the environment design makes very hard to test at all. The two-week job is to reframe around what was found, rewrite the prose in a standard register, add the analyses the existing logs already support, and run one cheap control.

## 1. The document does not yet read as a thesis

The text is full of sentences addressed to a writer rather than to a reader: "Numbers in this chapter are outcomes to interpret later. They are not followed by a claim." "Do not put unmatched epoch lengths in one versus-naive table." "Long jobs must use the centred JSON files." "They are not filled in here." "Related work may place ShapeLLM against a logistic chicken CPR. It may not write that…" "When the arm exists, three lines … come back from experiment chats." "Student-owned Results." "Not invented here." Alongside these is a private vocabulary that is never defined: lock, keep list, vault, aim column, photograph, star, tape, farm, smash, basin, licensed, LIT_CLOSE, criterion-4 pin, "F2-as-`verify_cpr.py`-wrote-it". Whatever the drafting workflow was, an examiner will read this as either an unfinished working document or as text produced under a rule-set that leaked into the output, and given the declaration on the title page you do not want that question in their mind. Every one of those sentences should be deleted or rewritten as a statement about the science.

Structurally, Chapters 6 and 7 both contain the results, largely duplicated, and Chapter 6 explicitly refuses to interpret them. The conventional split is design (conditions, seeds, hyperparameters, hypotheses, statistics, planned analyses) in Chapter 6 and numbers with interpretation in Chapter 7. Chapter 1 is a project-status memo, not an aims chapter: it needs research questions and a contributions paragraph, and it should not contain lists of which config files were executed. Chapter 9 is three paragraphs that repeat the abstract. The Limitations chapter uses a threat / "does not take down" format, which is a good idea, but every threat is dismissed, some by non-sequitur ("The threat is to over-reading, which Results already forbids" — a chapter cannot forbid a reader's inference). A limitations chapter in which no limitation limits anything is not doing its job; see Section 6 for the ones that do.

The abstract has the same problems in miniature (undefined terms, no research question stated, no interpretation, counts without denominators). Rewrite it last, from the new Chapter 9.

## 2. The environment is not the game the report says it is

Chapter 3 frames the game as "chicken, not prisoner's dilemma", with mutual take-1 (36 each) as restraint that "lasts the horizon", mutual take-2 as the death-open, and take-3 as "smash". That framing is a reduction to constant strategies, and the report's own DP numbers show it is misleading. From my implementation of your rules (harvest, scarcity split, then round-half-even logistic regrowth):

| Policy pair | Returns | Pool |
|---|---|---|
| (1,1) throughout | 36 / 36 | lives |
| (2,2) throughout | 7 / 7 | dies round 4 |
| (1,1) once, then (2,2) | 71 / 71 | lives, stock climbs to ~39 |
| (2,1) throughout | 72 / 36 | lives |
| (0,0) once, then (3,3) | 105 / 105 | lives, fixed point at R = 14 |
| (1,1) twice, then (3,3) | 104 / 104 | lives, fixed point at R = 15 |
| Best response to always-2 | 105 (open 0, then 3 forever) | lives, fixed point at R = 11 |
| Best response to always-1 | 107 (open 2, then 3 forever) | lives |
| Best continuation after a (2,2) opening, vs always-2 | 100 (0, 1, then 3s) | lives |

Four consequences follow.

First, mutual take-1 is not the cooperative outcome. It captures about a third of the joint optimum (210 total), and the joint optimum is reached by exactly one round of (0,0) followed by mutual take-3 forever. The action the report calls "smash" is the efficient sustainable action once the stock is above 14, and "restraint" is optimal for one to two rounds only. Every value-laden term in the results chapter (restraint, greed, smash-harvest, dove, farm) is calibrated to the wrong benchmark. In Test B, the whitened learner moving from take-2 toward take-3 (18% to 62%, survival 219/225) is moving toward the optimum; the centred learner staying at take-2 is further from it. The draft praises the second and deplores the first.

Second, the "death-open" is not a death sentence. After (2,2) at R = 8 the stock sits at 7, and a learner that takes 0 once recovers the pool and can still collect 100 of the 105 available against a frozen hawk. Your own Chapter 3 says the gap between opening 1 and opening 2 is "2 on a base of 104" and then makes opening the primary statistic anyway. Death requires the learner to keep taking 2 for three more rounds. So what the opening statistic actually measures is whether a near-constant policy chooses the one opening that a near-constant continuation can survive. The catastrophe is a property of the learner's policy class (constant, prior-dominated), not of the game. That is a legitimate object of study, but it must be stated that way.

Third, "chicken" is the right label for the constant-strategy reduction and the wrong label for the game. In the reduction, the payoff to a dove is 36 whether it faces a dove or a hawk (R = S), so being exploited costs nothing and no dove has any reason to retaliate; that is already a degenerate chicken, different from ShapeLLM's. Once you allow stock-conditioned continuations, the round-1 game against an opponent who will take 2 has values 105/104/102 for openings 0/1/2: there is essentially no dilemma left. The whole game is a one-round coordination problem at R = 8 followed by a trivial continuation.

Fourth, and this is the one that matters for the thesis title, the environment makes the shaping null almost inevitable. In the constant-strategy reduction the shaper's best policy against a learner who has learned to avoid death is to open 2 and stay on 2, which is also the untrained prior. There is nothing to teach and nothing to exploit beyond one unit in round 1 (72 versus 71). A ShapeLLM-style shaper only has work to do if the learner can be steered to a stock-conditioned build-up (a round of 0, then 3s), and neither agent in your runs ever learned to condition on stock. The two-phase teaching policy that Chapter 7 says was not observed is a straw man for this game; it would not be rational here.

What to do: rewrite Chapter 3 around the DP. Present the constant-strategy matrix as one reduction (this is what Leibo et al. 2017 call an empirical payoff matrix for sequential social dilemmas; cite that paper, it also justifies "sequential" in the title), then present the DP values and the stock-conditioned optimum, then state the knife-edge fact plainly: R₀ = 8 is one unit below the mutual-take-2 survival threshold of 9, and the design of the whole experiment rests on that unit. Then define the object of study honestly: does PPO on a peaked-prior LLM learn the one state-dependent decision that separates 7 from 71 (or 105)?

## 3. The advantage-normalisation finding is the real contribution and is under-analysed

The Test A contrast (whitened: never leaves opening 2, survival 28/225; centred: leaves on three seeds) is the most solid result in the report and it is currently described as "machinery, not a result". Promote it. But it needs an actual mechanism section, and the pieces are all in your logs.

The mechanism, as far as I can reconstruct it, is a sample-count effect. With π(2) ≈ 0.9 and π(1) ≈ 0.05, opening 2 is sampled roughly eighteen times more often than opening 1. The expected policy-gradient push on the opening logits is Σₐ π(a)·A(a)·∇log π(a); under whitening both openings kept positive advantages (+1.38 and +0.75), so both are reinforced and the frequent action wins on count, which is what you say in one clause ("the prior won on count") without doing the arithmetic. Under centring the frequent action's advantage went negative (−5.7), so the batch mean is doing the work of a baseline "relative to what the prior does". Write this out; it is a general and useful observation about PPO fine-tuning of LLMs with peaked priors and rare high-value actions, and it connects to Engstrom et al. 2020 and Andrychowicz et al. 2021 (which you should cite) on what normalisation choices do.

Three things undermine the current presentation. The A0 snapshots are not comparable: an affine transform preserves the ratio of differences, and (39.8 − 6.2)/(1.38 − 0.75) implies a batch standard deviation of about 53 and a batch mean of about −34, while the centred pair (16.7, −5.7) implies a batch mean of about +23 and a raw open-2 advantage of about 17, not 6.2. So the two "photographs" come from different batches with very different composition. Plot A0 by epoch for both modes rather than quoting two snapshots, and show the distribution of raw advantages in a batch so the reader sees where the −34 mean and the 53 spread come from. Second, the critic is close to untrained: vf_coef = 0.01, γ = 1, λ = 0.97, a fresh value head predicting 36-step returns in the range 0–105. Raw GAE at the opening is then close to "return minus a poor baseline", which is why later 2s carry large "smear". The whitening story is entangled with this and the report should say so; a quick ablation with vf_coef = 0.1 or 0.5 would tell you how much. Third, the whitened arm is a single seed against three centred seeds. Run whitened seeds 1 and 2; it is the same cost as anything else here and turns a 1-vs-3 anecdote into a 3-vs-3 contrast.

Also state the KL term. TRL 0.11 adds −β·KL(π‖π_ref) to the reward with an adaptive β (init 0.2, target 6 by default); since π_ref is the 90%-on-2 prior, this term directly penalises leaving opening 2. It is probably small relative to the return gaps (a few tenths per opening step) but it is part of the raw advantage and an examiner will ask.

## 4. The results chapter is missing the quantities that would settle its own questions

Returns are never reported. PPO maximises return, ShapeLLM measures shaping by return, and the hawk/dove language is about return, yet no per-agent return appears anywhere in Chapters 6 or 7. This matters most in the two-learner section: the "live mix at epoch 15 is still mostly 2s" for both agents, which means the "hawk–dove split" is very likely a 72-versus-71 split, a one-unit asymmetry in round 1. If so, "role lock" and "who doves" are dramatic names for a 1.4% difference. Report mean return per agent per epoch with intervals, and the reader can judge.

The policy as a function of stock is never shown. The records contain (R, action) for every step. A 4 × R table or heatmap of π(a | R) at the end of training, per condition, is the single figure that answers "did the learner learn the one state-dependent decision?", and it would also explain seed 2's preference for opening 0 (which the draft twice says it cannot explain).

No learning curves. Figure 6.1 is a first-versus-last bar chart and Figure 7.3 a last-three-epochs bar chart (I could not see the two external PDFs, 7.1 and 7.2); with 15 epochs and three seeds, plot the per-epoch opening share and survival with seeds as thin lines and the mean as a thick one. Figure 6.1 currently plots a bar for the whitened last epoch that you admit you do not have ("repeats that floor rather than inventing a lower point"). A placeholder bar with an apologetic caption is not acceptable in a thesis figure; the records exist, compute the number.

No uncertainty anywhere. Last-epoch counts are out of 15 games: 13/15 has a 95% Wilson interval of [0.62, 0.96], 9/15 of [0.36, 0.80]. Whole-run survival (134/225) is a strange statistic because it averages over the learning curve; report last-three-epoch survival with an interval instead, and keep whole-run only as a supplementary number. Three seeds is three seeds; say so once, in Limitations, with the consequence.

Vocabulary: define open-k, take-k, leave-2 once in Chapter 6 and use nothing else. Remove "star", "photograph", "tape", "farm", "smash".

## 5. The two-learner contrast cannot currently be read

The report already names the confound and then reports the contrast anyway. The naive agent updates every episode at LR 1.41 × 10⁻⁶ with the default clip range (0.2 in TRL, though the draft never says); the shaper updates once per five-episode trial at LR 3 × 10⁻⁷ with clip 0.1, on data up to five episodes stale. Whichever agent moves off the prior first relieves the death pressure on the other, so the faster learner doves and the slower one stays on 2. Under naive–naive the two are matched and symmetry-breaking decides; under naive–shaper the learning-rate asymmetry decides the same way in every seed. "Who doves did not swap" is exactly what that predicts with no shaping at all. The 50-epoch seed is consistent with the shaper barely moving (leave-2 at 3.3% over the run). The slow-LR naive–naive control that Chapter 6 lists as remaining is therefore not optional; it is the experiment. It costs the same as any other 3 × 15 run. Run it before anything else.

The info-off result (shaper leave-2 rises from 4–5% to 16–19%) is interesting but has a cheap alternative explanation: at LR 3 × 10⁻⁷ over 15 epochs the shaper's opening distribution is close to its prompt-conditioned prior, and the extra `transmit_info` text changes the prompt. Your `cpr_preflight.py` already measures the untrained action distribution; run it under both prompts. If the untrained prior already differs by that much, the effect is prompt, not learning.

Before any of this, state what a shaping success would look like in this game, in return terms, in Chapter 6: for example, shaper return above the hawk-role return in centred naive–naive across seeds, with intervals. Then Chapter 7 reports against that. With the Section 2 analysis in hand you should also say up front that a rational shaper here has at most one unit to gain, so a null is expected on this environment, and that the environment would need to be changed (larger action set relative to regrowth, or a stock-dependent temptation) before a shaping question is even well posed.

## 6. Limitations that actually limit

Replace the current chapter with the threats that do bite: the environment's efficient frontier is not take-1 and the opening statistic is downstream of a constant-policy assumption (Section 2); the shaping null is close to built in (Section 2, fourth point); the two-learner contrast is confounded by learning speed until the control is run (Section 5); the whitened arm is one seed; no returns or stock-conditioned policies are reported unless you add them; three seeds; the critic is near-untrained; some runs were on Apple MPS and some on CUDA (the report mentions MPS-specific illegal draws), which is a source of non-determinism worth one sentence; and the IPD reproduction gate (next section).

The public repo at HEAD does not contain the thesis: there is no `cpr_env.py`, `cpr_game.py`, `cpr_observation_managers.py`, `cpr_bots.py`, `logistic_cpr.py`, `finetuning_cpr.py`, `finetuning_cpr_fixed.py`, `scripts/run_cpr.sh`, no `configs/cpr_*.json`, no `tests/`, and no `results/` tree. What is there is the orphan float CPR, an RPS environment, and IPD reproduction configs. "The implementation is available at" is false today. Before submission the repo needs every file named in Chapter 5, every config named in Chapter 6, the per-run JSON summaries, and the analysis scripts that produce every figure and table from those summaries.

Your `THESIS_NOTES.md` records that the IPD reproduction gate for the ShapeLLM stack was closed as *not passed* on 6 July: the paper-style evaluation came out inverted, root-caused to `r1 == r2` in `environment.step()` plus an asymmetric evaluation mapping, inherited from upstream and not fixed. None of this appears in the report. A thesis that reuses a stack "with permission" and whose own notes say that stack did not reproduce the paper's headline result must disclose that, explain why the CPR path is unaffected (it has its own reward path in `cpr_game.py`, so the argument is probably clean), and confirm the diagnosis with Marta, who is both your supervisor and an author. Handled well this is a small contribution; omitted, it is the kind of thing that surfaces in a viva.
